# Annex A — Flood-control check dams with hours of storage

**Use this annex when the dam is built to cut flood peaks and trap sediment, and water stands for hours (not days or a permanent pond).**  
Groundwater is a **co-benefit**. Score flood/erosion and groundwater separately.  
Print with **Form E** (one sheet per flood). Keep the main protocol’s control wadi, wells, and karez (I4, I5).

`time wet after overflow stops ≈ residual volume ÷ (bed infiltration + leakage through the dam + any drain)`

While the flood still overtops the crest, extra inflow is spill. Most infiltration is on the **falling limb**.

---

## 1. Parameters that control hours of storage (checklist)

Staff complete the right-hand column on Form A (once) and confirm per flood on Form E.

| Group | Parameter | Longer ponding if… | Shorter ponding if… | Record |
|-------|-----------|--------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Structure | Crest / spillway height | Taller crest, more residual pool | Low crest, long overflow weir | m |
| Structure | Valley shape (area–volume) | Wide, flat wadi | Narrow V-gully | survey |
| Structure | Wall type | Masonry / tight earth | Gabion / leaky weir (drains through wall) | type |
| Structure | Low-level drain / notch | Closed or none | Open drain | Y/N |
| Structure | Cascade position | Most downstream, still receiving volume | Starved by upstream dams | 1, 2, 3… |
| Event | Flood volume vs storage | Storm fills the basin then recedes slowly | Storm far bigger than storage (brief overtop only) or too small to fill | peak stage |
| Event | Hydrograph shape | Snowmelt or long pulse | Short cloudburst | storm type |
| Event | Second peak same day | Refills before empty | Single pulse | Y/N |
| Event | Antecedent wetness | Already wet (slower i) | Dry gravel at season start (faster i) | note |
| Bed / aquifer | Bed texture | Silt / fine sand | Clean gravel | gravel/sand/silt |
| Bed / aquifer | Fresh silt drape | Thick drape | Scraped / coarse | cm |
| Bed / aquifer | Depth to water table | Table high, pond **connected** (infiltration slows) | Table deep, **disconnected** | m in nearest well |
| Catchment | Area and slope | Moderate catchment, storage not dwarfed | Steep large catchment, peak >> storage | ha, slope |
| Other | Vegetation, livestock, pumping | Roughness, no pumping | Trampling, pumping from pool | note |

Evaporation in **hours** of storage is usually small (~0.3–0.5 mm/h in a hot Kandahar afternoon). Still note it so emptying is not blamed on evaporation when the bed is gravel.

## 2. Extra measures for the assessment you will present

Do **not** rely on daily 07:00 “pond empty” logs. Use **events**.

| Add | Why | Staff task |
|-----|-----|------------|
| Form E, one per flood | Detention hours are the missing variable | Peak stage, overflow stop time, time dry (or next-morning stage) |
| High-water-mark nails or paint | Peak if nobody is on site | Mark after each flood; survey later |
| One pressure logger (10 min) **or** camera on the gauge **or** staff readings every 30–60 min on the falling limb | Flash floods are at night | Install on 1–2 dams |
| Gauge or crest-stage just **downstream** | Peak cut and lag | Ethiopia Tigray layout |
| Wells / karez at **1, 3 and 7 days after the flood** (treated vs control) | I4/I5 become the GW test | Same Form C IDs |
| Infiltrometer on wet bed next morning (2–3 floods) | Backup if logger misses overflow period | Technician |
| Silt grid when dry | Clogging dominates later years (Oman, Tunisia) | Form D after season |
| Photo points of banks / damage | Primary flood-control outcome | Same four photos every flood |

**Event infiltration (if falling limb is recorded after overflow stops):**  
`i (mm/h) ≈ hourly stage drop − E` then `I1_flood ≈ i × mean wetted area × hours wet`.

**KPIs for one season (two scorecards):**  
Flood: median hours wet; peak reduction or extra lag; silt trapped; structure standing.  
GW: I1 sum of events (if logger); **I4 extra well rise vs control after the same storm**; I5 extra karez days. A dam may pass flood control and show little I4 — report both.

Drop for this dam type: I2 “fillings per year” as a tank KPI; daily empty-pond MDWIR as the main number; isotopes/models until I4 is visible.

## 3. Reference projects (adjust the method from these, not from week-long recharge ponds)

| Place / activity | Structure | What they measured | Use in your method |
|------------------|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Arizona, USA** — leaky weirs, Cienega Ranch (Norman et al. 2025, *Appl. Water Sci.*) | Rock weirs **designed to leak** | 10-min loggers, cameras, crest-stage, soil moisture, GW; treated vs adjacent basins. Peak ~70% lower at one weir | Closest analogue; shallow alluvial water, not a regional aquifer |
| **USA gabions / NIDS** (Uhlman 2020; Fandel 2016; Norman 2019) | Leaky gabions | Isotopes, soil moisture | Wall leakage still recharges the bed |
| **Ethiopia, Tigray** (Guyassa et al. 2017, *J. Hydrol.*) | Gully check dams ± vegetation vs untreated | V-notch + sensors **upper and lower** 50 m. Peak delayed 44–61%; volume cut 8–18% vs 4–6% untreated | Copy two-section + control gully |
| **Tunisia, Koutine** (Ouessar / gabion ERT studies) | Gabions, ~200 mm rain | Sediment behind dams; SWAT | Same climate band; silt is the recharge killer |
| **Spain, SE ephemeral streams** (Martín-Rosales et al. 2007) | Many small dams | Infiltrometer + model; recharge 6–53% of inflow | Infiltrometer if no logger |
| **Cyprus, Peristerona** (Djuma et al. 2017, *Water*) | Check dam vs 11 km natural channel | Water balance at dam **and** on untreated bed. Dam ~30% of streamflow | Measure wetted channel, not only the pool |
| **Oman “dry dams”** (Al-Maktoum et al.; DPRI overview) | Detention, empty between floods | Infiltration tests over years. K from ~16 to 0.18 cm/h with silt | Put silt surveys in year 1 |
| **Morocco, pre-Saharan flood basins** (CIRAD farm MAR) | Basins that empty by infiltration | Loggers; 3 floods / 7 months. Local effect &lt; ~360 m; silt after each flood | Event balance; well radius |
| **China, Loess Plateau** (Huang et al. 2013; Shi et al. 2021, *J. Hydrol.*) | Sediment check dams | Table rose ~**35 days** after a big rain | Read wells at 1, 3, 7 **and ~30** days |
| **Iran, Gharah Kahriz** (SWAT / ECOPERSIA) | Check-dam network | Modelled runoff −43%, recharge +40% | Watershed story only; do not replace I4 |
| **Yemen, Sana’a** (Alderwish 2010) | Cascade of 2–4 m dams | Spreadsheet vs Darcy | Chain of small flood dams &gt; one high wall |
| **Pakistan gabions** (Kahlown & Abdullah 2004) | Leaky gabions into the bed | Slow release | Language: not a reservoir |
| **NCDOT, USA** (2014–17) | Rock checks in a swale | Hydraulic residence time | Detention hours as a standard KPI |
| **India MARVI / Dashora** (Rajasthan) | **Contrast:** ponds lasting days–weeks | Daily farmer gauges | Keep control wells and photo QA; **do not** use daily empty-pond logs as the core |

Also listed in WOCAT/FAO as gully checks, gabions, leaky weirs, jesour/tabias, wadi spreading.

**Cite in the method statement:** Arizona leaky weirs + Tigray upper/lower gauges + Cyprus dam-versus-channel + Oman silt + Morocco event loggers. State clearly that Dashora/MARVI is the source of well/karez QA, not of a seasonal pond water balance.
